Rules

The basics

Two teams take turns throwing metal boules, trying to land closer to the small wooden jack (the cochonnet) than the other side. Whichever boule sits closest when all boules have been thrown wins that end, scoring one point for every one of that team's boules that's closer than the opponent's single best boule.

A coin toss decides who throws the jack and picks the terrain for the match. Formats are Singles (3 boules each), Doubles (3 boules per player), and Triples (2 boules per player). First team to 13 points wins the match.

Within an end: whichever team is currently losing (farther from the jack) throws next, until one side is out of boules — then the other side plays out any boules they have left.

You can either Point (land a boule close to the jack) or Shoot (strike an opponent's boule to knock it away). A clean, direct hit that replaces the target boule almost exactly where it sat is a carreau — the ultimate shot.

Finer rules

The coin toss & captains

Team 1's captain chooses whether to flip the coin (letting the other team call it) or call it themselves (letting the other team flip). Whoever's call matches the result wins the toss, picks the terrain, and throws the jack first. In doubles/triples, a human captain can also choose which of their own teammates throws next and can call a shot (Point or Shoot) for them — a suggestion if both are human, but binding if either side of that call is a CPU.

Throwing the jack

The jack must land 6–10m from the circle and at least 0.5m short of the far end boundary to be valid. Each team gets exactly one throw of the jack — if it's invalid, the other team places it themselves (at a safe legal distance) and throws first from there, rather than getting a throw attempt of their own.

Circle placement

At the start of each end, the circle is placed over where the jack finally came to rest in the previous end (or its last valid spot, if it was knocked out of bounds). The circle can never be more than 10.5m from the string it's about to throw toward — if the natural spot would exceed that, it's automatically moved to comply. If there's room to spare, you can choose to pull the circle back for more throwing room, up to that same 10.5m limit.

When the jack goes dead

If the jack gets knocked out of bounds mid-end: if both teams still have unplayed boules, it's a dead end — no points scored, and the team that threw the jack this end throws it again, now in the reversed direction. If only one team still has boules left to play, they score points equal to exactly how many boules they had left unplayed. If neither side has boules left, no points are scored either way.

Exact ties

If measuring shows a genuine tie for closest boule, the team that threw the boule causing the tie throws again to try to break it. If it's still tied, it alternates to the other team, and so on until someone breaks it.

Time violations (cards)

Taking too long on your turn draws a warning first (yellow card) — the throw still goes through, just rushed. A second violation (orange card) disqualifies that throw outright. A third (red card) disqualifies your whole team from the match — no more leniency after that.

Spin

Every throw carries a natural amount of backspin by default, like real technique. You can adjust it before locking in your throw: drag up on the spin ball for topspin (the boule rolls further after landing), drag down for backspin (it holds its spot, or with enough of it at short range and a steep arc, can even roll back toward you), or drag sideways for side-spin (a lateral curve). Spin isn't applied with perfect consistency — the same setting won't produce identical results every time.

Terrain

Each surface has its own bounce (restitution), rolling resistance (friction), and unpredictability (chaos — the chance of a bad bounce off a stone). Harder, rockier terrain favors shooting; soft, fine terrain favors pointing.

The power & direction lock

After aiming, tap Throw to start a bar oscillating for power, then again for direction. Landing in the wide green zone gives a solid throw; landing in the narrow dark-green zone inside it gives near-perfect precision. When shooting, hitting the dark zone on both power and direction is what produces a clean carreau — missing the dark zone on just one axis still connects, but drifts slightly long/short or left/right depending on which one you missed.

Offline

1. A coin toss decides who throws first and who picks the terrain — your captain chooses to flip or call.
2. Tap the piste to choose your target spot, adjust arc with ▲ ▼, and drag the spin ball to add topspin, backspin, or side-spin.
3. Tap Throw, then time the power and direction bars to lock them in — the closer to the green zone, the truer the throw.
4. Whoever is farther from the jack throws next. Closest boules score a point each once all boules are played.
